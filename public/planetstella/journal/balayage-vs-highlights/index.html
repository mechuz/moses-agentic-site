Balayage vs. Highlights: Which Is Right for You?

By Stella · November 15, 2026

It's the most common question in the chair: should I get balayage or highlights? Both techniques lighten sections of your hair to add brightness and dimension. The difference is in how the lightener is applied, and that changes everything from the finished look to how often you'll need to come back.

What is balayage?

Balayage (from the French word for "sweeping") is a freehand technique. Lightener is painted directly onto the surface of the hair, usually starting a little below the root and getting brighter toward the ends. There are no harsh lines, so the result looks sun-kissed and natural.

  • Look: soft, lived-in, darker at the root and brighter at the ends
  • Grow-out: very forgiving. Many clients go 3 to 4 months between appointments
  • Best for: anyone who wants low-maintenance brightness, natural movement, or a gentle way to go lighter

What are foil highlights?

Highlights are woven or sliced sections of hair that are lightened inside foils. The foil traps heat, which allows for more lift and more precision, and the color typically starts right at the root.

  • Look: brighter, more uniform blonde with more contrast from root to tip
  • Grow-out: a visible line of regrowth appears sooner, so most clients refresh every 6 to 10 weeks
  • Best for: dramatic brightness, going significantly lighter, or a bright blonde all over

Balayage vs. highlights at a glance

  • Maintenance: balayage is lower; highlights need more frequent visits
  • Brightness: highlights can achieve more lift; balayage gives a softer gradient
  • Placement: balayage is painted where light naturally hits; highlights are placed in a pattern from the root
  • Grow-out: balayage blends in; highlights show a clearer line of regrowth

The secret: you don't have to choose

Some of the most beautiful blondes combine both techniques. A few babylights (very fine foils) around the face for brightness, with balayage through the lengths for softness and an easy grow-out. Stella looks at your natural level, your hair history and how often you want to visit, then builds a plan that blends the right techniques for you.

How to keep your blonde bright

  • Use a sulfate-free, color-safe shampoo and a purple shampoo once a week if brassiness creeps in
  • Book a gloss or toner between lightening appointments to refresh your tone
  • Always use heat protection, and add a bond-repair treatment to keep lightened hair strong

Not sure which is right for you? Book your balayage and bring your inspiration photos. Or go ahead and book highlights.
