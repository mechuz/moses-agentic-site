SAP reportedly weighs at least 10,000 job cuts as it reorganizes around AI; SAP says no decisions have been made

Bloomberg reported on November 26, 2026 that SAP is preparing to cut at least 10,000 positions as it reorganizes its software business for AI, with product, engineering and sales under particular review. In a statement to CNBC, SAP said AI is changing its business quickly and that no decisions on further measures have been made. SAP employs more than 110,000 people. CNBC notes that other software companies, including Atlassian and Cloudflare, tied job cuts to AI this year, and that Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 121,401 announced U.S. job cuts citing AI through October 2026, about 20 percent of the total.

Why it matters: Big software vendors are reorganizing around AI, which can change the products, support and account teams that smaller businesses depend on.

Tech by industry

JPMorgan and Hyundai Card test AI agents that transact with each other inside fixed spending limits

JPMorgan Chase and South Korea's Hyundai Card ran a proof of concept in which a Hyundai Card AI agent requested a simulated foreign exchange trade and a JPMorgan agent handled it on the bank's Kinexys blockchain network, with little human involvement. The companies plan a more complex phase with several interacting agents in the first half of 2027 and have set no date for a commercial product. JPMorgan says the hardest problems are accountability, governance and internal controls rather than the technology. Its framework keeps each agent inside preset transaction limits and automatically rejects requests above them.

Why it matters: Even large banks start agents with hard limits and clear human accountability, a sensible pattern for any business that lets software move money.

Software & devices

AI shopping agents are spreading, but Amazon and other big retailers are limiting or blocking them

AI shopping agents that buy on a shopper's behalf are getting more attention, but access is uneven. CNBC reports that Amazon has walled off its site and that eBay, United Airlines and Kohl's limit or block what agents can do, while Walmart and Sephora have opened up and Coach and Gap allow browsing but not checkout. In a Visa test of 22 models from 12 AI labs, agents failed about 43 percent of shopping runs, often by buying a product that did not meet the request. Bain & Co. puts agent purchases at about 0.4 percent of global e-commerce transactions. Who pays for an agent's mistakes is still unsettled.

Why it matters: Some customers will start arriving through software agents, so owners who sell online should decide whether to let them check out and how to handle their mistakes.

AI & automation

OpenAI releases GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, with lower costs per task for heavy and everyday work

OpenAI released two new models on November 17, 2026. GPT-6.2 Astra is built for demanding professional work such as documents, spreadsheets, code and research; GPT-6.2 Sol is a cheaper model for everyday tasks and multistep workflows. Both are available in ChatGPT Work and Codex on paid plans and in the API, priced at $8 and $40 per million input and output tokens for Astra and $2 and $10 for Sol. OpenAI says Astra completes a typical task for under a third of what its predecessor cost. On November 23, a Sol-based version began rolling out to the regular Chat tab, including Business plans.

Why it matters: The cost of a finished task keeps falling, so a team already paying for ChatGPT may get more work done on the same plan; check which model your workspace is set to use.

Small business tech

OpenAI pilots OpenWorks, free in-person AI help for independently owned U.S. small businesses

OpenAI started a pilot called OpenWorks on November 23, 2026 that pays local partners to help independently owned U.S. small businesses put AI to work, at no cost to the owner. Participating businesses also get three months of ChatGPT Business. Software and SaaS firms, venture-backed companies and private-equity-owned businesses are excluded; nonprofits and faith-based groups may apply. Partners are contractors who receive a stipend of up to $8,000 a month and must spend at least four days a month working in person with local businesses. The pilot starts in a limited set of U.S. communities, and owners elsewhere can register interest. OpenAI's help page does not say what happens after the free three months.

Why it matters: Free setup help lowers the cost of trying AI, but plan for what the tools will cost once the three-month trial ends.

Security, policy & law

New York bans the sale of AI chatbot toys for five years, starting in about 90 days

New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law on November 24, 2026 (S.9408A/A.11144B) that places a five-year moratorium on making, distributing and selling toys in the state with built-in AI chatbots, such as talking plush animals, dolls and robots that hold open-ended conversations with children over the internet. The law takes effect 90 days after signing, so it does not apply this holiday season. Violations carry civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day, enforced by the state Attorney General. The state pointed to consumer-watchdog testing in which some of these toys raised unsafe topics with children.

Why it matters: Toy and gift sellers that ship to New York should know which of their products include a chatbot before the ban takes effect in early 2027.

OpenAI lets businesses claim profiles in ChatGPT and publish their own agents there

OpenAI opened a beta Business Hub where companies can claim a profile inside ChatGPT and see how people engage with it. Eligible ecommerce and online service businesses can also publish a business agent that answers customer questions inside ChatGPT, and those agents can be promoted through ChatGPT ads. On November 19 OpenAI extended eligibility to businesses outside the U.S. TechCrunch describes the move as a step toward making ChatGPT work more like a search and discovery engine for local and online businesses.

Security, policy & law

New York's AI safety law starts putting large AI developers under state oversight

New York's RAISE Act, which covers the largest AI model developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic, is moving into effect, with developers required to register with the state by January 1, 2027. The law asks these companies to publish safety plans and report serious incidents, and it allows large penalties for violations. TechCrunch notes that California and Illinois have similar rules, while federal officials are pushing to limit how far states can regulate AI development.

Why it matters: The law targets the big model makers, not small businesses that use their tools, but it means more public safety information about the AI products you rely on.

Gartner: about one in five service leaders cut support staff because of AI, and many may rehire

A Gartner survey of customer service and support leaders found that 21 percent have reduced the number of human support agents because of AI. Most have not. Gartner also predicts that half of the companies that cut service staff because of AI will hire people back by 2027, as customers still expect a person for complex or sensitive issues and AI tools fall short of full replacement.

Why it matters: Use AI to take routine questions off your team's plate, but keep a clear path to a real person before you cut support staff.

Tech by industry

Study of 3,662 property managers: AI use reaches 58 percent, but under half see it in the bottom line

AppFolio and the Institute of Real Estate Management released AI Insights 2026 on November 5, 2026, a survey of 3,662 real estate management professionals taken May 7 to June 7, 2026. AI use in the job rose from 21 percent in 2023 to 58 percent, the first time a majority reported using it, and the smallest firms have passed 50 percent. Among users, 85 percent report productivity gains but only 48 percent report a positive effect on net operating income. Only 8 percent of organizations have a written AI policy and 74 percent of professionals have had no formal AI training. Concern is high: 92 percent worry about AI-related cybersecurity and 93 percent about AI-faked applicant documents.

Why it matters: Property managers who got results put AI into how the whole operation runs, not into one person's tasks; a one-page AI policy and basic training are the cheapest first steps.

Microsoft Ignite 2026: Copilot gets a long-running Autopilot agent, shared workspaces and a spend dashboard for agents

At its Ignite conference on November 17, 2026, Microsoft described the next version of Copilot for work: Autopilot, an agent that keeps running recurring tasks on its own; Copilot Code, which builds dashboards, trackers and small apps from plain-language instructions; and Copilot Workspaces, where people and agents share the same files and context. Security, policy & law

FBI: poisoned search results and hacked WordPress sites led to more than $28 million in ransom payments

The FBI issued an alert on November 16, 2026 about Gootloader, malware that reaches businesses through ordinary web searches. Criminals plant pages on hacked WordPress sites that rank for narrow searches, such as a specific contract clause, and the visitor downloads a file that opens the door for ransomware. As of October 2026 the FBI counted more than 1,000 infected WordPress sites, 216 domains run by the operator, and over $28 million in ransom payments. Recent targeting has focused on lawyers, in-house counsel and legal technology buyers. The alert tells organizations what to do if a staff member downloaded or opened such a file, and recommends reviewing incident response plans.

Why it matters: If your business runs a WordPress site, make sure it is updated and its XML-RPC access is locked down, and tell staff that a downloaded template or form from a search result is not safe to open.

Microsoft says a network upgrade, not Copilot, caused the Microsoft 365 outage

Outlook, Teams and Copilot were disrupted for many businesses starting November 12, 2026, and service came back gradually over the following days. Microsoft's preliminary explanation points to a hidden bug in the systems that set up secure connections, which surfaced when traffic shifted during network maintenance and upgrades. The company said its Copilot assistant did not cause the outage and that it saw no sign of malicious activity.

Why it matters: Email and chat can fail together when they share one provider, so agree in advance how staff and customers reach each other during an outage.

Infoblox confirms a security incident traced to an outside vendor

Infoblox, a company that sells network security products, confirmed that someone gained unauthorized access to certain company information. A spokesperson told TechCrunch the access came through a compromised third-party data migration vendor and that the company is investigating. The incident is a reminder that a breach can start with a supplier and not with the company itself.

Why it matters: Ask the vendors that handle your data how they protect it, and limit what each one can reach.

Apple services including the App Store and Apple Music go down; Apple points to an internal update

Several Apple services stopped working for part of November 17, 2026. Apple's status page listed full outages for Apple Music, the App Store and AppleCare, and reports named iCloud among the services hit.
